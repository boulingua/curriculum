# Analytic speaking rubric

Grounded in the CEFR Companion Volume (2020), **Appendix 3 — Qualitative features of spoken language (expanded with phonology)** (the CV's *Table 3*). The CV presents speaking quality as six analytic aspects; each is operationalised here by the CV illustrative scale it draws on, and the band descriptors are Boulingua's grounded Can-Do statements for that scale (traceable via `docs/descriptor-crosswalk.md`). No official rubric text is reproduced; bands are Boulingua reformulations.

| Aspect (Table 3) | Operationalising CV scale |
|---|---|
| Range | `LING.general-linguistic-range` |
| Accuracy | `LING.grammatical-accuracy` |

> **Interaction** here uses the pragmatic `turntaking` scale as the analytic proxy; the CV's Table 3 interaction column additionally draws on the interaction activity scales (domain `INT`), which are banded in the level files.

## C2

**Range**
- You draw on a full, dependable command of the language to say exactly what you mean, adding emphasis and fine distinctions with no sign of having to hold back.

**Accuracy**
- I keep my grammar accurate in complex language even while my attention is on something else, such as planning ahead or watching how others react.

**Fluency**
- You keep going at length with an easy, natural flow, pausing only to pick the perfect wording or a fitting example.

**Coherence**
- You weave a whole text into a seamless unit, drawing on many structuring patterns and a broad set of cohesive devices exactly where each fits.

**Phonology**
- I command the whole sound system of the language with precise control, so any accent carried over from my other languages never blurs even the subtlest points of my message.
- I pronounce virtually every sound of the language clearly and precisely.
- I use stress, rhythm and intonation deftly to signal fine distinctions of meaning and to add emphasis.

## C1

**Range**
- You pick a fitting wording from a wide repertoire and handle complex grammar flexibly, expressing yourself clearly without limiting what you want to say.

**Accuracy**
- I sustain a high level of grammatical accuracy, making only rare errors that are difficult to notice.

**Fluency**
- You express yourself smoothly and spontaneously, almost without effort; only a genuinely demanding subject briefly slows your flow.

**Interaction**
- I can pick the right ready-made phrase to grab the floor, or to buy time and keep it while I think.

**Coherence**
- You produce clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured language, keeping firm control over organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices.
- You build a well-organised, coherent text using a variety of cohesive devices and structuring patterns.
- You deploy a variety of linking expressions efficiently to signal clearly how your ideas relate to one another.

**Phonology**
- I draw on the full range of sound features well enough to stay fully intelligible; a slight accent from my other languages may show but never gets in the way.
- I articulate nearly all sounds with a high degree of control and usually catch and fix a slip when I clearly mispronounce one.
- I speak smoothly and clearly, with only occasional slips in stress, rhythm or intonation that don't reduce how well I am understood.
- I vary my intonation and place stress accurately to say exactly what I mean.

## B2

**Range**
- You have enough language to describe things clearly, give viewpoints and develop arguments with little visible word-searching, using some complex sentence forms to do so.

**Accuracy**
- I control grammar well; my occasional slips and minor flaws in sentence structure are rare, and I can often correct them afterwards.
- I show a fairly high degree of grammatical control and avoid mistakes that would lead to misunderstanding.

**Fluency**
- You handle even long, complex passages spontaneously, often with striking fluency and ease of expression.
- You keep a fairly steady pace; you may hesitate while searching for patterns and phrasing, but you rarely pause for long.
- You interact fluently and spontaneously enough that regular conversation with fluent speakers works without tiring either side.

**Interaction**
- I can step into a discussion at the right moment with the right words.
- I can open, carry and close talk smoothly, trading turns effectively.
- I can start talking, take my turn and end a conversation when I need to, even if not always gracefully.
- I can fall back on set phrases to buy time and hold my turn while I work out what to say.

**Coherence**
- You connect your points into clear, coherent discourse with a limited set of cohesive devices, though a long stretch may feel a little jumpy.
- You write text that is generally well-organised and coherent, using a range of linking expressions and cohesive devices.
- You divide longer texts into clear, logically ordered paragraphs.
- You bring a counter-argument into a simple discursive text, for example flagging it with a word like 'however'.

**Phonology**
- I generally use fitting intonation, correct stress and clear individual sounds; my accent shows but has little or no effect on being understood.
- I pronounce a high proportion of sounds clearly across longer stretches of speech and stay intelligible throughout, despite a few consistent mispronunciations.
- I can work out how most unfamiliar words should sound, such as where the stress falls, with reasonable accuracy, for example while reading.
- I use stress, intonation and rhythm to reinforce the message I want to convey, though some influence from my other languages remains.

## B1

**Range**
- You can cope with unexpected situations, explain the main points of an idea or problem fairly precisely, and share thoughts on abstract topics such as music or film.
- You have enough vocabulary to manage familiar topics like family, hobbies, work, travel and current events, though you hesitate, paraphrase and repeat yourself when words run short.

**Accuracy**
- I handle simple structures well and some complex forms too, though I tend to use the complex ones rigidly and not always accurately.
- I communicate with reasonable accuracy in familiar situations; errors and noticeable first-language influence occur, but it stays clear what I mean.
- I use a stock of frequent routines and set patterns tied to more predictable situations with reasonable accuracy.

**Fluency**
- You express yourself with relative ease and keep going effectively on your own, despite pauses and dead-ends while formulating.
- You stay understandable even while pausing very visibly to plan grammar and vocabulary and self-correct, especially in longer stretches.

**Interaction**
- I can join a discussion on a familiar topic by using a set phrase to get the floor.
- I can start, keep going and end a simple face-to-face conversation about familiar or personal topics.

**Coherence**
- You string together shorter, separate simple points into a connected, straight-line sequence.
- You form longer sentences and join them with a few cohesive devices, for instance when telling a story.
- You make simple, sensible paragraph breaks within a longer piece of text.

**Phonology**
- My pronunciation is generally intelligible, with word- and sentence-level stress and intonation that don't stop my message getting through, even though my accent reflects my other languages.
- I stay generally intelligible even though I regularly mispronounce individual sounds and words I am less familiar with.
- I get my message across understandably despite a strong influence on stress, intonation and rhythm from my other languages.

## A2

**Range**
- You can handle routine everyday situations with predictable content, though you usually have to simplify your message and search for words.
- You produce short everyday phrases to meet simple practical needs, such as giving personal details, describing daily routines, stating wants, or asking for information.
- You use basic sentence patterns and set phrases to talk about yourself and other people, what you do, places and belongings.
- You rely on a small stock of memorised phrases for predictable survival situations, and communication often breaks down when things are unfamiliar.

**Accuracy**
- I use some simple structures correctly but still make basic mistakes systematically; even so, it is usually clear what I want to say.

**Fluency**
- You get your point across in short turns, though pauses, false starts and reformulations are very obvious.
- You build phrases on familiar topics easily enough for brief exchanges, despite clear hesitation and false starts.

**Interaction**
- I can use basic techniques to begin, keep up or end a short conversation.
- I can start, sustain and finish a simple face-to-face conversation.
- I can get someone's attention when I want to speak.

**Coherence**
- You use the most common connectors to link simple sentences, telling a short story or laying out a plain list of points.
- You join groups of words or signs with simple connectors such as 'and', 'but' and 'because'.

**Phonology**
- I speak clearly enough to be understood, though listeners occasionally ask me to repeat; strong influence from my other languages on stress and rhythm can affect me, but familiar words come out clearly.
- I am generally understandable in simple everyday situations, as long as the listener makes an effort with specific sounds.
- My consistent mispronunciations don't stop me being understood, provided the listener makes an effort to adjust to my language background.
- I handle the stress and intonation of everyday words and phrases understandably, despite strong influence from my other languages.
- My stress patterns are good enough for familiar everyday words and simple utterances.

## A1

**Range**
- You have a handful of simple expressions covering basic personal details and concrete needs.
- You form short one-clause sentences with a few basic structures, sometimes leaving out or shortening words.

**Accuracy**
- I show only limited control of a few memorised simple grammatical structures and sentence patterns.

**Fluency**
- You manage very short, mostly ready-made phrases, pausing a lot to find expressions, articulate unfamiliar words/signs, and repair communication.

**Coherence**
- You link words or signs, or short groups of them, with very basic linear connectors like 'and' or 'then'.

**Phonology**
- I can be understood, with some effort, on a very small set of learned words and phrases, and I reproduce a limited range of sounds and stress for simple familiar items.
- I can reproduce sounds of the language when carefully guided.
- I articulate only a few sounds, so I am understood only when the listener supports me, for instance by repeating correctly and prompting me to try the sounds again.
- I manage the stress and intonation of a small set of simple words and phrases understandably, though very strong influence from my other languages means my listener needs to be collaborative.

## preA1

**Range**
- You use single words or signs and a few fixed expressions to give simple information about yourself.

**Accuracy**
- I can put words or signs in a very simple order within short statements.

**Fluency**
- You produce a few very short, rehearsed phrases, relying on gestures and signalling for help when you get stuck.
